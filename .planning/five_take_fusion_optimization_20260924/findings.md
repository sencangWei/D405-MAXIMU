All five frontends used the same VINS-camera local-motion and opt-in metric-keyframe config. Lighthouse was read only for the final independent score. The selected pre-v6 reference is `.planning/imu_scale_orientation_20260924/selected/take{2,4,5}/precision.json`, **not** the old `fusion/sparse` intermediate.

This candidate fixes the MASt3R late local scale problem in take1 and largely in take3 but is **not** a universal improvement. On takes2/5 the original visual frontends' short/long scales were mutually close (0.326/0.336, 0.361/0.360); v6 introduces the inconsistent ratios. Do not relax the 5% cross-window gate or enable v6 by default. Selected take2's remaining 14.29mm peak is sustained at the tail; both selected fusion and VINS errors there point in similar directions. The selected 35–38s fusion short-stereo vector residual P95 is 3.37mm, not the 7.24mm measured on an **unselected sparse branch**. In 35–38s, 29 accepted non-overlapping 0.1s stereo edges cover ~2.9s: cumulative stereo displacement versus external GT differs by about `[-0.2,+1.7,+3.0]mm`, whereas VINS differs `[-4.9,-2.3,+0.2]mm` on those exact edges. This is a *diagnostic*, not permission to optimize against GT. The graph already includes stereo displacement factors; adding equivalent window-sums would double-count the same measurements unless a new independent constraint or different upstream state is introduced.

Take3's 25.30–25.83s excess is 17 contiguous external samples; final fusion max 11.87mm. D405 short-edge residual P95 there is ~2.9mm and VINS-only also shows a 25–26s external error peak, so neither single-frame interpolation nor simply increasing a stereo edge weight has a causal justification. The tracker stream has ordinary ~7.9ms sample spacing and no obvious velocity spike in that window. An external GT/extrinsic issue is a possibility, not proven.

- Existing selected stage-6 scale repair yielded full trajectories only for fresh take2/take4/take5. Position maximums: 14.29/6.07/8.56 mm. Full acceptance FAIL on take2 position and fresh rotation gates.
- Fresh take1 and take3 stopped at dense 10 Hz stereo scale (`relative_p90_p10=0.849` and `0.642` respectively); no fusion precision is available.
- All five VINS acceptance reports PASS. Take1/take3 have two independent stereo summaries already PASS and mutually close: bidirectional/long-hops 0.29729/0.28959 and 0.30764/0.31017 m per MASt3R unit; the dense short-hop report alone FAILS and prevents the multisecond stage from running. This is an upstream optional-sensor-quality issue, not proof the whole recording is bad.
- **Correction to the first hypothesis:** Dense 10 Hz dispersion in take1/take3 is not isolated random short-hop stereo noise. Accepted local stereo/MASt3R ratios rise persistently from about 0.28–0.31 to about 0.54–0.58 over the last 10–15 s, across all 1/2/3-hop groups. Median D405 metric displacement stays centimeter-scale while the corresponding MASt3R displacement shrinks. Other takes are much steadier. The dense report is correctly detecting a likely MASt3R *time-varying visual scale/shape failure*; simply ignoring the FAIL would conceal a real front-end error.
- Bidirectional and long-hop reports independently show the same late scale doubling, despite both returning global-scale PASS. Matching stereo observations against `trajectory_online_frames.csv` and final `trajectory_frames.csv` gives virtually identical late ratios (take1 about 0.59, take3 about 0.55). Thus the distortion is already present in MASt3R's online pose stream, not introduced by the later graph/fusion or final global batch export. The existing graph code has `local_stereo_scale_state`, but report validation requires global PASS; diagnostic ablation with the two valid reports will test whether its local constraints are sufficient.
- Diagnostic graph replay for take1/take3 using the two already-PASS stereo reports and the new attitude-source scale selector runs numerically, but the independent input-quality gate REJECTS both (`primary_shape_not_independently_supported`): stereo edge RMSE 6.11/9.48 mm (limit 4 mm), fusion input disagreement P95 84/180 mm. Graph requested max position corrections 54/93 mm but the current 25 mm per-node cap clipped 90/294 frames. This confirms skipping the dense failure does **not** recover valid data, and the existing local stereo state alone does not absorb this large warp.
- Visual inspection of IR frames near the scale transition: take1 moves from a cluttered desk at frame 750 toward a close monitor/desk with broad dark or planar surfaces at frame 1050; take3 also changes view markedly between frames 750 and 1050. These views plausibly reduce visual geometry, but the images alone do not prove the scale-drift mechanism. Take4 frame 1050 retains multiple textured scene objects; no deterministic visual-quality conclusion without feature/pointmap diagnostics.
- Lighthouse is scoring-only; the five sessions are development data and cannot establish generalization on their own.
- Preflight of the existing D405 stereo-depth provider on 15 frames (150/750/1050 from each take) found 15–37% finite pixels at the MASt3R target resolution. Even take1 frame 1050, the weakest sampled frame, has 30,316 valid depth pixels; median depth 0.327 m. This rules out total absence of stereo depth but does not establish reliability of its absolute scale. The frozen front-end config disables `stereo_pointmap_scale_prior` and `stereo_fix_pose_scale`; the first isolated A/B enables only pointmap scale prior.
- Take2's selected-candidate 14.29 mm position maximum is not a brief spike: >10 mm persists for the final ~2 s, and 5-s median ATE climbs from ~1 mm early to 11.13 mm in the last bin. A frame-interpolation spike fix is inappropriate. The diagnostic global Sim3 scale of ~0.95 is consistent with accumulated scale mismatch, but this is not yet a causal proof.
- Take2/4/5 selected fusion position-aligned rotation RMSEs are 2.236/3.436/5.436 degrees, versus independent attitude-aligned 0.751/1.643/1.826 degrees. The disagreement between position-optimal and attitude-optimal global alignment is 2.106/3.017/5.120 degrees. Their position trajectory cross-covariance smallest/largest singular-value ratios are 0.067/0.027/0.009, respectively: the more near-1D the track, the more position-only SE(3) alignment leaves a poorly constrained rotation. The reported rotation-gate FAIL may partly be an alignment/evaluation observability issue rather than local IMU attitude failure; do not change the metric or claim PASS without a defined common-frame criterion.
- Cross-sensor displacement check at accepted dense-stereo pairs, with VINS body pose converted to left-IR camera using `body_T_cam0`: take1 stereo/VINS median is 0.996 at 25–30 s and 0.970 at 35–40 s, while MASt3R/VINS drops 3.462→1.683. Take3 stereo/VINS is 0.985 at 0–20 s and 0.988 at 35–40 s, while MASt3R/VINS drops 3.273→1.631. Take4 stereo/VINS remains ~0.96–0.99 and MASt3R/VINS ~3.5–3.7. This strongly localizes take1/3's scale change to MASt3R's visual trajectory rather than stereo geometry or fixed camera lever arm (though VINS and stereo are not fully independent sensors).
- Front-end take1 A/B with only `stereo_pointmap_scale_prior=True` completed. It does **not** correct the local warp: candidate/original relative displacement stays 0.256 in early and late bins, and Sim(3) fit to original has only 0.062 mm RMSE (0.59 mm max). The selected stereo/visual ratio still rises 1.08→2.29. Therefore this switch alone changes the global gauge, not the trajectory shape. Rejected as a product improvement; testing the separately meaningful combination with fixed pose scale next.
- Front-end take1 with pointmap prior **and** fixed Sim(3) pose scale improves the accepted stereo/visual median from 0.277→0.585 across late bins to 0.960→0.978. But it creates 20–60 mm single-frame position jumps at 34–39 s while simultaneous VINS motion is 1–5 mm. The existing dense stereo-scale gate returns PASS (relative p90–p10 0.303) because many jump-corrupted pairs are rejected by direction/motion tests before its dispersion summary. This is a **false-positive quality result**; no production rollout. Next test adds existing metric-depth anchoring and guarded keyframe PnP, with independent frame-step sanity check.
- Existing metric-depth-anchor + guarded keyframe-PnP front-end A/B failed more severely: online pose has 58.4 mm one-frame maximum, full batch export becomes NaN from source frame 832 (~27.7 s), and relocalization fails after frame 1190. It therefore cannot be converted into a valid full trajectory. The metric PnP reported >90% inliers on many frames but was unavailable at frame 870/1050 when only 22/12 candidate metric-depth points survived; good average inlier counts do not guarantee coverage through the bad interval.
- Prototyped a 2.5 s sliding-median local stereo scale correction **without changing product code**. Take1/3 visual-vs-VINS SE(3)-aligned RMSE fell from 49/63 to 11/15 mm, but P95 remained 27/39 mm; take2 worsened from 3.3 to 4.8 mm. A plain position-domain window scale is not a safe universal solution. Do not ship this prototype.
- Added a conservative full-rate isolated-step continuity gate after stereo scale estimation. Five baseline front-end tracks pass with max metric steps 9.4–13.9 mm; the hard-fixed-scale candidate fails with seven isolated jumps and max 59.7 mm. This prevents a future summary PASS from promoting that class of broken trajectory, but does not itself improve ATE.
- Stronger internal sensor cross-check: transform VINS body pose to left-IR camera and compare accepted D405 PnP displacement **vectors** in camera-i coordinates, not merely their lengths. Across the five takes, stereo-versus-VINS vector residual P95 is 3.23/2.54/4.17/4.34/4.47 mm, with median direction cosine ~0.999 in each. The 2× late visual-scale change on take1/take3 is therefore not explained by stereo/VINS disagreeing with each other; a continuous-window metric factor backed by both onboard sensors is justified. This agreement is not an external accuracy guarantee because both sensors share D405 images and calibration.
- 2026-09-25 opt-in one-second VIO displacement window prototype, gated by local D405 stereo/VIO vector agreement, was exercised on **all five** takes without Lighthouse in the estimator. A frame-gauge bug in the first candidate made take1's stereo/VIO vector residual 27.8 mm median because the VINS-to-MASt3R frame was obtained from position-only SE(3) on an almost 1-D visual trajectory. The onboard-attitude frame alignment reduced take1 median to 2.87 mm. Position-derived versus attitude-derived global rotation differed by 83.82° on take1 and 20.06° on take2 (2.78/2.52/2.09° on takes3/4/5). This is a real observability/coordinate-alignment issue, not a Lighthouse or TCP input.
- The corrected window candidate is nevertheless **rejected** by five-take internal A/B. Stereo-edge RMSE baseline→candidate (mm): take1 6.11→13.35, take2 4.06→4.12, take3 9.48→10.38, take4 3.26→3.11, take5 3.59→3.26. Correction-clipped frames baseline→candidate: 90→154, 152→166, 294→301, 0→234, 0→209. Attitude alignment alone (no added window edges) still clips 178 and 79 frames on the previously unclipped takes4/5. Thus sensor-frame correction is necessary to reason correctly but **not sufficient** to improve this graph under its existing visual prior and 25 mm correction guard. No production trajectory or parameter was changed; do not promote these experimental factors or bypass the guard to claim improved precision.
- Isolated MASt3R tracker `sigma_depth` experiment (10→0.05, other tracking and global BA settings frozen) completed on take1's full 1199 frames. This weights the learned log-depth pointmap, **not** D405 metric depth. D405 dense stereo still reports FAIL, scale dispersion 0.849→0.862, and accepted stereo/visual median scales in frames 750–900 / 1050+ change 0.278/0.578→0.282/0.581. No causal improvement; do not expand this single-switch experiment to the other four takes. Outputs isolated under `frontend_depth_tracking_v1_take1/`, no production config changed.
- Added a diagnostic-only MASt3R online Sim(3) scale sidecar and replayed baseline take1/take3/take4. In **all three**, both full and online SE(3) output CSVs compare byte-for-byte identical to their original baseline, so the sidecar did not change trajectories. Take1's Sim(3) median changes ~0.987 (frames750–900) →0.648 (1050+), while stereo/visual scale changes 0.278→0.578. Take3's Sim(3) median 0.881→0.190 (750–900 vs900–1050), stereo/visual 0.321→0.544. However good take4's Sim(3) median varies 0.466→1.082→1.362 over the same bins while stereo/visual stays 0.271→0.270→0.262. Therefore a raw Sim(3) scale drop correlates with failure on take1/3 but is **not sufficient or necessary** for metric trajectory failure; hard fixing the scale is contradicted by the good-take counterexample and earlier jump experiment. Next cause-directed investigation must separate learned pointmap scale, pose translation update, and accepted correspondence geometry before choosing an upstream intervention.
- A second diagnostic-only frontend trace records D405 depth / MASt3R pointmap-Z ratio on every third frame, per-frame accepted match count, keyframe anchor, and relative Sim(3) pose for full baseline replays of take1/3/4. The online and final trajectories of **each of these three** replays are byte-identical to the earlier baseline; the trace did not perturb output.
- The two failing takes show a sustained D405 displacement / visual displacement increase **within the same keyframe**, not only across keyframes: take1 same-keyframe median 0.276 (frames750–900) →0.562 (1050–1200), take3 0.318→0.555. Cross-keyframe pairs are somewhat worse (take1 late 0.620, take3 late 0.607) but cannot alone explain the distortion. The good take4 stays 0.267→0.262 for same-keyframe pairs and 0.274→0.258 for crossing pairs. The downstream reanchoring/loop stage is not the sole cause; a bad relative visual tracking update is already present.
- Simple pointmap-scale or match-count thresholds do **not** separate the bad and good takes. In take1 the median D405/pointmap-Z factor rises 0.262 (frames450–750) →0.402 (1050–1200); in take3 it changes 0.314 (750–900) →0.121 (900–1050) while the stereo/visual motion ratio increases in both. Good take4 has even larger pointmap factor changes, 0.131 (750–900) →0.375 (1050–1200), with a nearly constant stereo/visual motion ratio. The Spearman correlation between sampled pointmap scale and accepted stereo/visual displacement ratio is +0.315/-0.341/-0.131 for takes1/3/4 respectively. All three also have lower match fractions during motion, so match count alone is not the discriminator. This rules out a simple clamped pointmap scale or match-fraction gate as a safe fix. It does not rule out learned geometry/reprojection bias interacting with the tracker; identifying that interaction needs a new, separately verified residual-level test.
- The stereo PnP observations remain mostly healthy where the visual ratio fails: take1 frames1050–1200 accept 136/147 sampled pairs, median inlier ratio 0.810 and reprojection P95 1.458 px; take3 frames900–1050 accept 133/150, inlier ratio 0.859 and reprojection P95 1.189 px. Together with prior stereo/VINS vector agreement, this makes an explanation based solely on stereo PnP outliers less likely. These are not independent GT measurements, so this is still an inference, not proof of absolute accuracy.
- A residual-level soft prior on relative Sim(3) scale, enabled only with D405 metric pointmaps, was tested on the complete take1 frontend and rejected. The baseline accepted D405/visual ratio in frames 750–900 and 1050–1200 was 0.277/0.566; candidate ratio was 1.085/2.206. The soft prior avoided the hard-fix jump (candidate max one-frame step 8.01 mm), but only changed gauge and did not remove the relative-displacement magnitude drift. The temporary tracker code was removed. This reinforces that the next candidate must explicitly constrain local camera-frame metric displacement, not pointmap scale or Sim(3) scale in isolation.

Within several fixed-keyframe intervals, final motion is only 0.414–0.468× online. The online output has four 26–63 mm keyframe seam jumps; a naive VINS keyframe reanchor increases some jumps to 24–98 mm.
